## 👋 Hey, I'm Matt

I like taking an idea from a blank page to something that actually runs — not just picking a framework and gluing tutorials together, but figuring out the architecture, the data flow, the weird edge cases, from the ground up. 🛠️ Give me a vague problem and I'll come back with a working prototype and three opinions about how to make it better.

I don't stick to one lane. One week it's computer vision, the next it's a rule engine reacting to sensor events, the next it's parsing legal text or messing with audio waveforms. 🔀 If it's a system I haven't built before, that's usually reason enough to try.

### Some favorites

**[NyAI](https://github.com/Dheeraj-Murthy/NLP_PE)** *(ongoing)* — teaching models to parse legal documents by their actual structure, not as generic text.

**[Kaizen](https://github.com/MJTheGreat3/Kaizen)** *(ongoing)* — a local-first life tracker for my laptop and phone: coding practice and GitHub, gym and sports, habits, focus timer and more, end-to-end encrypted and synced between devices with no server of its own.

**[Vestique](https://github.com/MJTheGreat3/Vestique)** — multimodal fashion search: YOLO spots the garment, BLIP2 describes it, CLIP embeds it, closest match retrieved.

**[Traffic Management System](https://github.com/Dheeraj-Murthy/dm)** — C++ simulator + Go backend + a SQL-backed rule engine reacting to sensor/signal events in real time.

**[CRAS](https://github.com/MJTheGreat3/CRAS)** — Contamination Risk Analysis and Alert System, models contamination spread over a hydrology network.

**[Alexa+ MCP](https://github.com/MJTheGreat3/Alexa-_MCP)** — MCP server that turns meeting notes into coordinated action across Linear, Notion, and Slack, driven by an agent reasoning over the transcript.

**[Audio Editor](https://github.com/Dheeraj-Murthy/AudioEditor)** — a Java-based editor for poking at waveforms when I run out of visual/text data to mess with.

### How I work

Fast at prototyping, comfortable being the person who says "let's just build it and see" — and just as comfortable throwing out an approach that isn't working and trying the next one. Adaptability isn't a buzzword for me, it's what happens when you keep picking projects outside your comfort zone on purpose. ⚡
